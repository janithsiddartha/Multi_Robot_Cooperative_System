There are three closely related methods

A. Null-space motion / self-motion
Keep the TCP position fixed while joints move into different configurations. The TCP orientation is allowed to change.
This is the simplest method for what you're describing.

B. Fixed-position orientation control
Keep the TCP position fixed while deliberately changing its orientation. This is a constrained inverse-kinematics problem.
The robot must have sufficient kinematic capability to achieve the requested rotation.

C. Circular Cartesian trajectory
Move the TCP along a circular arc around a specified pivot point. The TCP position changes along the circle.
This is different from rotating the tool while keeping its TCP position fixed.
